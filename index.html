    ENVIRONMENTAL SCIENCE

    Forest Fires — Causes, Effects & Prevention

    Detailed reference material for the EVS component of the project.

    🔥

    What is a forest fire?

    A forest fire is an uncontrolled fire that spreads through vegetation such as grass, shrubs, leaves and trees. Fire requires heat, fuel and oxygen, while weather, vegetation and terrain influence ignition and spread.

    ⚡

    Natural causes

    • Lightning can ignite dry vegetation.
    • Heat and drought can make vegetation highly combustible.
    • Rare natural events such as volcanic activity can provide ignition.
    🚬

    Human causes

    • Unattended campfires and outdoor burning.
    • Discarded cigarettes or hot materials.
    • Agricultural or waste burning spreading into vegetation.
    • Electrical faults, machinery sparks and vehicles.
    • Deliberate burning or arson.
    🌡️

    Conditions that increase risk

    • High temperatures dry vegetation.
    • Low humidity reduces moisture in fuels.
    • Low soil and vegetation moisture indicate dry conditions.
    • Strong winds can accelerate spread and carry embers.
    • Continuous dry vegetation provides available fuel.
    🌳

    Environmental effects

    • Loss of vegetation and wildlife habitat.
    • Smoke and particulate pollution.
    • Greater soil erosion after vegetation loss.
    • Carbon stored in biomass is released during combustion.
    • Severe fires can alter ecosystem structure.
    🛡️

    Prevention & preparedness

    • Follow local restrictions on outdoor burning.
    • Never leave campfires unattended.
    • Keep ignition sources away from dry vegetation.
    • Manage dry fuel and maintain firebreaks where appropriate.
    • Use early-warning systems and rapid reporting.
    • Combine weather, satellite, sensor and ground information.

    Why early warning matters

    Identifying dangerous environmental conditions early can give forest managers more time to investigate, issue warnings and organize preventive action. Forest Guardian demonstrates this concept by turning geographic and environmental data into an understandable risk level.
